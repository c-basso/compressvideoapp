How to compress a video on iPhone

Updated October 4, 2026 · 4 min read

Quick answer

iPhone has no built-in “compress” button for an existing video. Open it in Compress Video, optionally trim it, drag the resolution slider down in Compress settings, tap Compress and then Download Compressed. You get a smaller HEVC copy; the original stays untouched.

Phone videos are huge: a minute of 4K is easily more than 150 MB. That breaks email, chat apps and uploads, and fills storage fast.

Photos can trim a clip but cannot re-encode it to a smaller size. Below are the built-in options, what they really do, and the four-tap way to compress any video on iPhone.

What iPhone can do without an app

None of the built-in options compress a video you already recorded. They either change future recordings or hand the job to a service.

  • Settings → Camera → Record Video: lowers the size of new recordings only.
  • Settings → Camera → Formats → High Efficiency: records future videos in HEVC instead of H.264.
  • Photos → Edit: trims the clip, but the remaining part keeps its original bitrate.
  • iMovie: exporting at a lower resolution works, but it means building a project for one clip.
  • Online compressors: they require uploading a private video to someone else's server.

Why on-device compression matters

Compress Video encodes the clip on your iPhone. The video is not uploaded to a server to compress it, there is no account, and nothing leaves the phone until you share it yourself.

How the resolution slider works

Compress Video scales resolution with a slider instead of fixed presets. It shrinks width and height proportionally, so the aspect ratio never changes.

Pixel count drops with the square of the slider: 50% keeps a quarter of the pixels. From a 4K clip, 50% is about 1080p and about a third is about 720p. From a 1080p clip, about two thirds is about 720p.

Which settings make the biggest difference

Resolution has the biggest effect: going from 4K to 1080p cuts the pixel count by four. Frame rate comes next: 60 fps to 30 fps roughly halves the frames to store. Audio is small compared with video, but muting it helps when you only need the picture.

Compress a video on iPhone with Compress Video

Free download, works offline, about a minute for a typical clip.

  1. 1

    Open the video in Compress Video

    Open Compress Video and pick the clip from Photos or Files, or tap Share on the video in Photos and choose Compress Video.

  2. 2

    Trim what you don't need

    Tap Trim Video, drag the handles to keep only the part you need, and tap Save. Shorter clips are smaller clips.

  3. 3

    Choose how small

    Open Compress settings. Drag the resolution slider down, lower the frame rate if needed, and set audio to compress, keep or mute.

  4. 4

    Compress and check the size

    Tap Compress. The app shows the new size and how much smaller it is. Flip Original and Compressed to compare.

  5. 5

    Save or share

    Tap Download Compressed to save the smaller copy to Photos, or share it straight to Mail, WhatsApp, Discord or any app.

Tips for the smallest file

  • Trim first: every second you remove is saved at 100%.
  • For chat apps, about half the original resolution is usually enough on a phone screen (4K at 50% ≈ 1080p).
  • Keep the original until you have checked the compressed copy.

Questions people also ask

Is there a way to compress a video on iPhone for free?

Yes. Compress Video is a free download with a daily number of compressions included. iOS itself can only lower the quality of future recordings, not existing ones.

Can I use an iPhone Shortcut to compress a video?

Shortcuts can encode media, but you have to build and tune the shortcut yourself and you don't see the before and after size. A dedicated app gives you resolution, frame rate and audio controls plus a size comparison.

Does compressing replace the original video?

No. Compress Video saves a new, smaller copy. The original stays in Photos until you delete it.
